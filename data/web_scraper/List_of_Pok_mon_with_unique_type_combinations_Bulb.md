# List of Pokémon with unique type combinations

From Bulbapedia, the community-driven Pokémon encyclopedia.

This is a **list of Pokémon with unique type combinations** across the generations. A Pokémon qualifies as having a unique type combination if no other Pokémon aside from any evolutionary relatives has that type combination. Since the game rarely makes a distinction between a Pokémon's first and second types when calculating damage, this list does not consider every permutation of types, but instead considers only combinations. In other words, Pokémon such as Spiritomb (a Ghost/Dark-type Pokémon) and Sableye (a Dark/Ghost-type Pokémon) are treated as having the same type combination.

## Trivia

- As of Generation IX:
  * 21.0% of all (162) used type combinations are unique to a single Pokémon (18 combinations) or evolutionary line (16 combinations).
  * Zubat and Golbat are the oldest Pokémon with unique type combinations, spanning back since Generation I. Meanwhile, the oldest single Pokémon with a unique type combination is Tyranitar, spanning back since Generation II
  * Zubat, Golbat, and Crobat are the only Flying-type Pokémon to have a unique type combination, while Snover and Abomasnow are the only Grass-type Pokémon with this distinction.
- Several single-type Pokémon—Tangela, Dratini, Dragonair, Sudowoodo, Umbreon, Misdreavus, and Flying-type Arceus—were unique in their single typing when introduced, but later generations introduced more Pokémon with those types.
- Generation V introduced the most Pokémon with unique type combinations, with 32.
- Generation IX introduced the fewest Pokémon with unique type combinations, with 9.
- Mega Altaria is the only Mega-Evolved Pokémon with a unique type combination to not be shared with its base form, though Mega Sceptile previously held this title as well prior to the introduction of Alolan Exeggutor.
- Dratini, Dragonair, Nincada, and Surskit are the only Pokémon that are not fully evolved to have had unique type combinations not shared by their fully evolved forms.
  * Sandy Cloak Wormadam is the only fully-evolved dual-type Bug/Ground Pokémon.
  * A fully evolved pure Dragon-type Pokémon would not be introduced until Generation V, while Dratini and Dragonair lost their unique status in Generation III with the introduction of Bagon and Shelgon.
- While not evolutionary relatives, Carbink and Diancie are the only dual-type Rock/Fairy Pokémon; however, some Pokémon that are otherwise related to each other had unique type combinations:
  * The eon duo (Latias and Latios) were the only dual-type Dragon/Psychic Pokémon until the debut of Ultra Necrozma in Pokémon Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon, released during Generation VII.
  * Lunatone and Solrock (which are often considered counterparts) were the only dual-type Rock/Psychic Pokémon until the debut of Iron Boulder in The Indigo Disk DLC expansion for Pokémon Scarlet and Violet, released during Generation IX.
- Several Pokémon had a unique type combination until the introduction of specific Pokémon introduced during a generation.
  * These Pokémon had a unique type combination until the release of Pokémon Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire, released during Generation VI:
    + Numel and Camerupt were the only dual-type Fire/Ground Pokémon until the debut of Primal Groudon.
    + Pirouette Forme Meloetta was the only dual-type Normal/Fighting Pokémon until the debut of Mega Lopunny.
    + In Pokémon X and Y, Inkay and Malamar were the only dual-type Dark/Psychic Pokémon until the debut of Hoopa Unbound.
  * Dragalge was the only dual-type Poison/Dragon Pokémon until the debut of Naganadel in Pokémon Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon, released during Generation VII.
  * Hawlucha was the only dual-type Fighting/Flying Pokémon until the debut of Galarian Zapdos in The Crown Tundra DLC expansion for Pokémon Sword and Shield, released during Generation VIII.
  * These Pokémon had a unique type combination until the release of Pokémon Legends: Arceus, released during Generation VIII:
    + Frillish and Jellicent were the only dual-type Water/Ghost Pokémon until the debut of Basculegion.
    + Mow Rotom was the only dual-type Electric/Grass Pokémon until the debut of Hisuian Voltorb and Hisuian Electrode.
    + Diggersby was the only dual-type Normal/Ground Pokémon until the debut of Ursaluna.
    + Croagunk and Toxicroak were the only dual-type Poison/Fighting Pokémon until the debut of Hisuian Sneasel and Sneasler.
    + Amaura and Aurorus were the only dual-type Rock/Ice Pokémon until the debut of Hisuian Avalugg.
    + Togetic and Togekiss were the only dual-type Fairy/Flying Pokémon until the debut of Enamorus.
  * These Pokémon had a unique type combination until the release of The Teal Mask DLC expansion for Pokémon Scarlet and Violet, released during Generation IX:
    + Galarian Slowbro and Galarian Slowking were the only dual-type Poison/Psychic Pokémon until the debut of Munkidori.
    + Galarian Weezing was the only dual-type Poison/Fairy Pokémon until the debut of Fezandipiti.
    + Prior to Pokémon Scarlet and Violet Version 2.0.1, Scovillain was the only dual-type Grass/Fire Pokémon until the debut of Hearthflame Mask Ogerpon.
    + Lotad, Lombre, and Ludicolo were the only dual-type Water/Grass Pokémon until the debut of Wellspring Mask Ogerpon.
    + Lileep and Cradily were the only dual-type Rock/Grass Pokémon until the debut of Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon.
  * Gastly, Haunter, and Gengar were the only dual-type Ghost/Poison Pokémon until the debut of Pecharunt in The Indigo Disk DLC expansion for Pokémon Scarlet and Violet, released during Generation IX.
  * Terrakion was the only dual-type Rock/Fighting Pokémon until the debut of Mega Barbaracle in Pokémon Legends: Z-A, released during Generation IX.
  * Helioptile and Heliolisk were the only dual-type Electric/Normal Pokémon until the debut of Stereo Rotom in Pokémon Pokopia, released during Generation IX.
- In Generation VI, several existing Pokémon had their type changed to become Fairy-type. If these Pokémon had been Fairy-type from the beginning, it would have resulted in several unique type combinations:
  * Clefairy and Clefable would have been the only pure Fairy-type Pokémon until the debut of Togepi, Snubbull, and Granbull in Generation II.
  * Jigglypuff, Wigglytuff, and Igglybuff would have been the only dual-type Normal/Fairy Pokémon until the debut of Azurill in Generation III.
  * Mr. Mime would have been the only dual-type Psychic/Fairy Pokémon until the debut of Ralts, Kirlia, and Gardevoir in Generation III.
  * Mawile would have been the only dual-type Steel/Fairy Pokémon until the debut of Klefki in Generation VI.
  * Registeel would have been the only pure Steel-type Pokémon until the debut of Klink, Klang, and Klinklang in Generation V.
